Multi-node training: gang scheduling and all-reduce

Why jobs need all-or-nothing placement, and what the network does

NOT: Training jobs are not independent Pods you can place one at a time.
IS: A tightly coupled group of ranks that must all run, on a fast fabric, or none should.

Why this mattersWhy does this matter? Training jobs are not like web servers. All the workers must run together, or none can make progress. If the scheduler places only some of them, they hold expensive GPUs and wait forever. This is a classic way platforms waste money, and a very common interview scenario.
  1. Without gang scheduling the default scheduler places Pods one by one.🛺 Wedding party: some guests are seated, the rest have no chairs.
  2. Cluster state: 8 GPUs, 3 busy, only 5 free. The job needs 8.
  3. Five workers get placed immediately and start holding GPUs.
  4. Three workers stay Pending.
  5. The job cannot start (collectives need every rank) yet 5 GPUs are locked. Waste and starvation for others.
Why this mattersThe fix is all or nothing admission. Kueue keeps the job in a queue until every worker can start together, and it also gives each team a fair share of GPUs. For a director level interview, this is your fairness and utilisation story: how many teams share a scarce fleet without fighting.
  1. With Kueue the whole job is one Workload, admitted all-or-nothing.🛺 Book the whole wedding hall or none of it.
  2. Same cluster: 3 busy, 5 free. Job needs 8.
  3. Not enough quota or capacity → the Workload stays queued. No Pods hold GPUs; other teams can use the 5 free ones.
  4. Three busy GPUs finish and free up. Now 8 are available.
  5. Kueue admits the Workload; all 8 Pods start together. No partial placement, no deadlock.
Why this mattersWhy does this matter? In multi-node training, the network is as important as the GPUs. If the fast fabric is misconfigured, the job silently falls back to slow networking, and you pay for GPUs that wait. When you can explain this simply, you show you understand why GPU platforms are really network and scheduling problems.
  1. Data-parallel training: every GPU computes gradients, then NCCL averages them (all-reduce).🛺 Four friends each add a column of a bill, then pass the partial sums around until all know the total.
  2. Forward + backward: each GPU has its own local gradients.
  3. Reduce inside a node first over NVLink (fast, 100s of GB/s).
  4. Then across nodes over EFA / InfiniBand with GPUDirect RDMA (NIC reads GPU memory directly).
  5. Every GPU now holds the same averaged gradients; next step begins.
  6. If the fabric is misconfigured (wrong NIC, no EFA, cross-AZ) NCCL silently drops to TCP. Debug with NCCL_DEBUG=INFO.
